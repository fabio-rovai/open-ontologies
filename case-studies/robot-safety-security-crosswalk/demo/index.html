Robot safety and security crosswalk, by autonomy band

A cyber compromise of a robot is a safety event. This page lets you pick a band of the autonomy gradient, see the exposures that band creates, and see, for each exposure, the safety clause and the security requirement that address it. Every row carries the kind of evidence behind it and how strongly it is held. Rows that are our own reasoning are marked as ours and look different from rows carried by a cited normative reference.

The chain, hop by hop

The widely marketed claim is that ISO 10218:2025 mandates IEC 62443. That is false as stated, and the falsification is free: the complete Normative references clause of every document in the chain is published in the official preview. What ISO 10218:2025 mandates is a cyber security threat assessment. Everything downstream of that is guidance. Here is each hop, with its status established by reading the reference list itself.

    5. How to read a row

    Two badges appear on every row. The first says what kind of evidence supports the mapping, ordered below from strongest to weakest. The second says how strongly it is held, which is a judgement about the evidence trail and not about how sure anyone feels. Clause identifiers themselves were all read in an official free preview unless a row says otherwise; the badge grades the join between the safety side and the security side, which is the part that can be wrong.

    Body clauses of an ISO or IEC deliverable are normative by the ISO/IEC Directives, and annex status is printed on the annex heading, so it is recorded per annex here. Do not assume annex letters mean the same thing across the two parts of ISO 10218:2025: Part 1 Annex H is normative (stopping time and distance measurement), Part 2 Annex H is informative (means of verification and validation). Note also that IEC TS 63074:2023 is a Technical Specification, not an International Standard, and that nothing obliges anyone to conform to it unless a contract or another document invokes it.

    6. Honest scope, and what could not be confirmed

    ISO and IEC standards are paywalled. No normative text from any ISO or IEC standard is reproduced on this page. What is recorded here is the public surface: designations, part numbers, editions, publication dates, clause identifiers, clause titles, annex letters and annex normative status, all of which the issuing bodies and their distributors publish free in cover pages, contents pages, forewords, scopes and normative-reference lists. That is a design feature: a crosswalk built from public surfaces alone is reproducible by any reader without buying anything.

    Two mapping annexes were deliberately not retrieved: IEC 62443-3-3:2013 Annex B Table B.1 and IEC 62443-4-2:2019 Annex B Table B.1, which map requirements to security levels. Both are informative and both are paywalled. The table identifiers are cited; no cell content is.
